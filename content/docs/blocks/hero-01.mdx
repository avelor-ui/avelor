## What flips in RTL

- The two columns swap sides on wide screens: the text sits on the start side and the product frame on the end side, through grid flow alone.
- All text aligns to the start edge, and the call-to-action links keep their reading order, so the primary link comes first in both languages.
- The arrow in the primary link is marked `data-rtl="flip"` and mirrors with `rtl:-scale-x-100`.
- The placeholder frame mirrors as a whole (`data-rtl="flip"`): its bars start at the start edge and the tinted action pill sits at the end (`ms-auto`). Nothing in it is transformed.

## Reduced motion behaviour

On load, the eyebrow, heading, paragraph and links reveal one after another (a short upward fade, about half a second in total), and the frame reveals with the heading. Only vertical movement is used, so the entrance is the same in both directions. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, or with the Motion control set to Reduced, everything renders in its final state immediately. Without JavaScript the content is fully visible through a `noscript` style.

## Customise

- The link targets live in `content.ts` (`#get-started`, `#demo`); point them at your own pages.
- Replace the placeholder frame with your product image.
- The heading is an `h1`, so use one hero per page.
